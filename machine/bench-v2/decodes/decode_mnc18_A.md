On Tuesday, March 10, the Métropole de Lyon presented its cycling plan for 2026-2030. The budget is €186 million: the State contributes €42 million, the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region €15 million, and the Métropole covers the rest. The plan includes 120 km of new separated bike lanes, 35 km of them on the Rhône and Saône quays, and 8,000 secure bike parking spaces near stations. Thomas Girard, vice-president for mobility, said works will start in September 2026 with the Part-Dieu–Villeurbanne line, which is 6.4 km long. According to 2025 counts, the bicycle's share of home-to-work trips rose from 4% to 7% in five years; the goal is 12% in 2030. Opposition member Claire Morel criticized it as « un plan qui oublie les communes de l'ouest ». Girard replied that 30% of the new kilometres will be outside Lyon and Villeurbanne. A public consultation will run on the Métropole's website from March 16 to April 30 inclusive; associations may also submit written contributions until May 15. The final vote by the metropolitan council is planned for June 22.
