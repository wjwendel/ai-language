## §2 How-to (en): Repotting a houseplant

**English**

1. Get a new pot that is 2 inches wider and has a drainage hole in the bottom.
2. Water the plant one day ahead. Moist soil holds the roots together, which makes the plant easier to remove.
3. Lay down newspaper to protect the table.
4. Tip the old pot onto its side, gently hold the plant at the base of the stem, and slide it out. If it's stuck, tap the sides of the pot or run a butter knife around the edge.
5. Use your fingers to loosen the root ball. Trim any roots that are black, mushy, or smell bad, because those roots are rotting.
6. Put fresh potting mix in the bottom of the new pot, so that the top of the root ball sits 1 inch below the rim.
7. Center the plant and fill the gaps with mix. Press lightly so there are no air pockets, but don't pack it down hard.
8. Water slowly until water drains out of the bottom.
9. Keep the plant out of direct sun for about a week while it recovers, and don't fertilize it for a month.

## Ambiguities and key gaps

### Readings I had to choose

**§1**
1. **"Daniel drive + groceries return."** I read this as Daniel driving Leo to the game and buying groceries on the way back. Two other readings are possible: Daniel drives and then returns with the groceries, or someone else handles the groceries.
2. **"birthday cake Grandpa = 70 Mon."** I read this as Grandpa turning 70 on Monday. It could also mean the birthday itself is on Monday. The payload doesn't say whether Grandpa is one of "my parents," so I didn't assume he is.
3. **"? !fixed @12 > call plumber."** I read "@12" as "by noon." The key defines @HMM only as a clock time, not as a deadline, so "by" vs. "at" is my guess.
4. **"number fridge."** I read this as "the plumber's number is on the fridge." The key has no locative ("on/at") operator.
5. **Who does unassigned tasks (recycling, vacuuming, faucet).** I assigned them to "I" because the key says plan actor = I and no actor = default.

### Things the key didn't explain
- **Title text after the §N header** (e.g. "§2 howto en repot houseplant"). The key defines `§N type lang` but not the topic words that follow.
- **The colon in a list** ("groceries return: eggs…"). The key's `X:` rule means "X said / X handles," which clashes with an ordinary list colon here.
- **Comma.** Not defined. I treated it as a list or clause separator.
- **`<` used two ways.** It means "because" in `<` alone and "under" in `<N`. Only spacing and context tell them apart.
- **`@HMM` with three digits** (@630 → 6:30). Not spelled out. The key also doesn't say whether a bare @H like @12 means "at" or "by."
- **"meanwhile," "until," "before," "after," "within," "next month."** These appear as plain words, not operators. That's fine, but it shows the key isn't exhaustive.
- **News tense.** The key's "news = past" rule doesn't cover current status or future plans that appear inside news items.
- **Unused unit tags.** `y`, `date`, `day`, and `line` are listed. Only `date`, `day`, and `line` show up in the payload; `y` is never used.
- **No modals or imperatives** (can't / should / must / don't). These all had to be inferred from `!` and context.
- **No possessive or locative markers** ("number fridge," "Grandpa cake").
