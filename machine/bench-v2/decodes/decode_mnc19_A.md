On Tuesday, March 10, the Métropole de Lyon presented its 2026–2030 cycling plan, worth €186 million, of which the State contributes €42 million, the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region €15 million, and the Métropole the rest. The plan includes 120 km of new separated bike lanes, 35 km of them along the Rhône and Saône quays, as well as 8,000 secure bicycle parking spaces near stations.

According to the vice-president for mobility, Thomas Girard, works will begin in September 2026, starting with the 6.4 km Part-Dieu–Villeurbanne route. The share of home-to-work trips made by bike rose from 4% to 7% over five years (based on 2025 counts); the goal is 12% by 2030.

Opposition member Claire Morel criticized « un plan qui oublie les communes de l'ouest ». Girard replied that 30% of the new kilometres will be outside Lyon and Villeurbanne.

A public consultation will run on the Métropole's website from March 16 to April 30 inclusive; associations may submit written contributions until May 15. The final council vote is planned for June 22.
